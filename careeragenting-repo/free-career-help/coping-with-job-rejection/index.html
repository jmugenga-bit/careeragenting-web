Coping with job rejection and staying motivated

Rejection is one of the hardest parts of looking for work, and getting knocked back again and again can chip away at anyone. It's not a sign that something's wrong with you. Here's what genuinely helps.

WWren · CV SScout · applications AAce · interviews JJuno · support CCedar · growing in the job

Rejection isn't a verdict on you

Most rejections come down to fit, timing, or simply a lot of strong applicants for one role — not your worth as a person. Employers often can't interview everyone good. It's normal to feel low about it; it's also worth remembering that one 'no' says very little about the next opportunity.

What actually helps

A few things reliably make the search more bearable: applying for fewer, better-matched roles rather than blanketing everywhere; taking real breaks so it doesn't consume you; talking to someone rather than carrying it alone; and marking small wins — a tailored CV, a first-round interview — not just outcomes.

Turn feedback into your next step

Where you can, ask for feedback and treat it as information, not judgement. Sometimes it's a small, fixable thing — a clearer CV, stronger interview examples. Preparing more thoroughly for the next one often changes the result, and it puts you back in a position of doing something rather than waiting.

Frequently asked questions

How do I stay motivated when job hunting?

Apply for fewer, better-matched roles, take real breaks, mark small wins (a tailored CV, a first interview), and talk to someone rather than carrying it alone. Preparing well for the next opportunity helps you feel back in control.

Why do I keep getting rejected?

Often it's fit, timing or a large number of strong applicants — not a reflection of your worth. Where you can, ask for feedback; sometimes a small, fixable change to your CV or interview answers makes the difference.

Does Career Agenting System offer emotional support?

Its companion, Juno, offers encouragement and a steady presence through the ups and downs of job hunting. It isn't therapy or a crisis service — for that, please speak to your GP or a support line like Samaritans (116 123).

How do I ask for interview feedback?

Reply politely to the rejection, thank them, and ask if they can share any brief feedback to help you improve. Not every employer will, but it's worth asking and treating any answer as useful information.
