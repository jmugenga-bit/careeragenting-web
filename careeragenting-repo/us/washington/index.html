Just laid off in Washington? Here's your playbook.

Seattle's tech giants hire — and cut — in waves. Whether you're at Amazon, Microsoft, Boeing or a startup, this is a clear, step-by-step guide for Washington: what to do first, the money and rights you're owed, and how to line up your next role. The first 72 hours after a layoff

Do these four things and you'll be on solid ground.

  • Get your final paycheck. In Washington, your final wages are due by the next regular payday — it doesn't have to be same-day, but any earned, unused paid time off owed under your employer's policy should be paid out too.
  • File for unemployment right away. Washington pays the highest unemployment benefit in the US — up to $1,208 a week (minimum $383) for new claims from July 2026 — for up to 26 weeks. And there's no state income tax in Washington, so more of it stays with you. File at esd.wa.gov.
  • Sort out health cover. A job loss lets you enroll outside open enrollment through Washington Healthplanfinder, or continue your employer plan via COBRA — compare the real costs first.
  • Don't rush to sign severance. It usually asks you to give up the right to sue. You're allowed to take time to read it — and to have it reviewed — before signing.

Your rights when you're let go in Washington

  • At-will, with limits. Washington is at-will, but you can't be fired for an unlawful reason — discrimination or retaliation. The Washington State Human Rights Commission handles those complaints.
  • Layoff notice. Washington has no separate state WARN law, but the federal WARN Act can require larger employers (100+ staff) to give 60 days' advance written notice of a big layoff or plant closing. If a mass layoff caught you with no notice, it's worth checking.
  • Severance isn't required by law — it's discretionary. Weigh what you'd give up against what you'd get.

General information, not legal advice. For your situation, use the official Washington resources below or an employment attorney.

Money and leverage while you search

  • Claim unemployment — it's the most generous in the country (above). Report any part-time or gig work; you may still get a reduced benefit.
  • Use Washington's pay-transparency law. Under the Equal Pay and Opportunities Act, employers with 15 or more employees must include the pay range and a general description of benefits in every job posting. Use those numbers to target the right roles and negotiate from fact.

Your résumé for the Washington market

Seattle-area hiring is ATS-first. Mirror the posting's exact keywords, keep formatting clean (no tables or columns the software can't read), lead with achievements and metrics, and skip the photo, birth date and age.

Frequently asked questions

How much is unemployment in Washington State, and for how long?

Washington pays the highest unemployment benefit in the US — up to $1,208 a week (minimum $383) for new claims from July 2026 — for up to 26 weeks, and there's no state income tax. File at esd.wa.gov.

When do I get my final paycheck in Washington?

By the next regular payday after you leave, including any earned, unused paid time off owed under your employer's policy.

Do Washington employers have to post the salary?

Yes. Under the Equal Pay and Opportunities Act, employers with 15 or more employees must include the pay range and a general description of benefits in every job posting.

Does Washington have a state WARN law for layoffs?

No separate state WARN law — but the federal WARN Act can require larger employers (100+ staff) to give 60 days' advance notice of a mass layoff or plant closing.

Is this legal or financial advice?

No. It's general information, current as of August 2026 and based on the official Washington sources on this page. For your own situation, check those sources or speak with a qualified attorney or advisor.
