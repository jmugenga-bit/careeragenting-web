Just laid off in Massachusetts? Here's your playbook.

Boston's biotech and tech scene runs hot — and layoffs come with it. This is a clear, step-by-step guide for Massachusetts: what to do first, the money and rights you're owed (including some of the most generous unemployment in the US), and how to line up your next role. Free to use, and honest about information versus advice.

The first 72 hours after a layoff

  • Your final paycheck is due the same day. Under the Massachusetts Wage Act, if you're laid off or fired your employer must pay everything you're owed — including accrued, unused vacation, which Massachusetts treats as earned wages — on your last day. (If you quit, it's due by the next regular payday.) Late pay can carry stiff penalties.
  • File for unemployment right away. Massachusetts is among the most generous — about 50% of your average weekly wage up to $1,105 a week, plus a dependency allowance of up to $25 per child, for up to 30 weeks (longer than most states). File at mass.gov/unemployment.
  • Sort out health cover through the Massachusetts Health Connector (a job loss lets you enroll outside open enrollment) or continue your plan via COBRA.
  • Don't rush to sign severance — it usually asks you to give up the right to sue. Take time to read it, and have it reviewed, first.

Your rights when you're let go in Massachusetts

  • At-will, with limits. Massachusetts is at-will, but you can't be fired for an unlawful reason — discrimination or retaliation — handled by the Massachusetts Commission Against Discrimination (MCAD).
  • Layoff notice. Massachusetts relies mainly on the federal WARN Act, which can require larger employers (100+ staff) to give 60 days' advance written notice of a mass layoff or plant closing.
  • Severance isn't required by law — it's discretionary.

General information, not legal advice. For your situation, use the official Massachusetts resources below or an employment attorney.

Money and leverage while you search

  • Claim unemployment — it's among the most generous in the US, and lasts up to 30 weeks (above). Report part-time or gig income and you may still get a reduced benefit.
  • Use the new pay-transparency law. Since 29 October 2025, employers with 25 or more employees must include the pay range in job postings. Use those numbers to target the right roles and negotiate from fact.

Your résumé for the Massachusetts market

Boston hiring — especially in biotech and tech — is ATS-first. Mirror the posting's exact keywords, keep the formatting clean, lead with achievements and metrics, and skip the photo, birth date and age.

Frequently asked questions

How much is unemployment in Massachusetts, and for how long?

About 50% of your average weekly wage, up to $1,105 a week, plus a dependency allowance of up to $25 per child, for up to 30 weeks — longer than most states. File at mass.gov/unemployment.

When do I get my final paycheck if I'm laid off in Massachusetts?

The same day. Under the Massachusetts Wage Act, employees who are laid off or fired must be paid everything owed — including accrued, unused vacation — on their last day. If you quit, it's due by the next regular payday.

Do Massachusetts employers have to post the salary?

Yes. Since 29 October 2025, employers with 25 or more employees must include the pay range in job postings.

How much notice do I get before a layoff in Massachusetts?

Massachusetts relies mainly on the federal WARN Act, which can require larger employers (100+ staff) to give 60 days' advance written notice of a mass layoff or plant closing.

Is this legal or financial advice?

No. It's general information, current as of August 2026 and based on the official Massachusetts sources on this page. For your own situation, check those sources or a qualified attorney or advisor.
