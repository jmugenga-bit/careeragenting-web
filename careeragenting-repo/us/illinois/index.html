Just laid off in Illinois? Here's your playbook.

Chicago is a powerhouse for finance, tech and healthcare — and the layoffs land here too. This is a clear, step-by-step guide for Illinois: what to do first, the money and rights you're owed, and how to line up your next role. Free to use, and honest about information versus advice.

The first 72 hours after a layoff

  • Your final paycheck is due by the next regular payday in Illinois, including earned wages and any vacation owed under your employer's policy.
  • File for unemployment right away. Illinois pays up to about $628 a week — with extra allowances if you have a dependent spouse or child — for up to 26 weeks. File at ides.illinois.gov.
  • Sort out health cover through Get Covered Illinois (a job loss lets you enroll outside open enrollment) or continue your plan via COBRA.
  • Don't rush to sign severance — it usually asks you to give up the right to sue. Read it, and have it reviewed, first.

Your rights when you're let go in Illinois

  • At-will, with limits. Illinois is at-will, but you can't be fired for an unlawful reason — discrimination or retaliation — handled by the Illinois Department of Human Rights.
  • Layoff notice. Under the Illinois WARN Act, employers with 75 or more full-time employees must give 60 days' advance written notice of a mass layoff or plant closing — on top of the federal WARN Act.
  • Severance isn't required by law.

General information, not legal advice. For your situation, use the official Illinois resources below or an employment attorney.

Money and leverage while you search

  • Claim unemployment the day you're let go — and remember Illinois adds allowances for a dependent spouse or child, so don't skip that in your claim.
  • Use Illinois's pay-transparency law. Since 1 January 2025, employers with 15 or more employees must include the pay scale and benefits in job postings. Use those numbers to target the right roles and negotiate from fact.

Frequently asked questions

How much is unemployment in Illinois, and for how long?

Illinois pays up to about $628 a week, with extra allowances if you have a dependent spouse or child, for up to 26 weeks. File at ides.illinois.gov.

Do Illinois employers have to post the salary?

Yes. Since 1 January 2025, employers with 15 or more employees must include the pay scale and benefits in job postings.

How much notice do I get before a layoff in Illinois?

Under the Illinois WARN Act, employers with 75 or more full-time employees must give 60 days' advance written notice of a mass layoff or plant closing.

When do I get my final paycheck in Illinois?

By the next regular payday after you leave, including earned wages and any vacation owed under your employer's policy.

No. It's general information, current as of August 2026 and based on the official Illinois sources on this page. For your own situation, check those sources or a qualified attorney or advisor.
