Just laid off in New York? Here's your playbook.

From Wall Street to Silicon Alley, New York hires deep and cuts fast. This is a clear, step-by-step guide for New York State — what to do first, the money and rights you're owed (including some of the strongest layoff-notice rules in the country), and how to line up your next job. Free to use, and honest about information versus advice.

The first 72 hours after a layoff

  • Your final paycheck is due by the next regular payday in New York, including any earned wages owed.
  • File for unemployment right away. New York recently raised its maximum benefit sharply — from $504 to up to $869 a week — payable for up to 26 weeks. File online through the New York State Department of Labor.
  • Sort out health cover through the NY State of Health marketplace (a job loss lets you enroll outside open enrollment) or continue your plan via COBRA.
  • Don't rush to sign severance — it usually asks you to give up the right to sue. Take time to read it, and have it reviewed, before signing.

Your rights when you're let go in New York

  • At-will, with limits. New York is at-will, but you can't be fired for an unlawful reason — discrimination or retaliation — handled by the NY State Division of Human Rights (and, in the city, the NYC Commission on Human Rights).
  • Some of the strongest layoff notice in the US. New York's own WARN Act requires employers with 50 or more employees to give 90 days' advance written notice — a full month more than the federal 60 days — before a mass layoff (25+ employees who are a third of the site, or 250+), a plant closing, or a relocation. If a big layoff gave you less, it's worth checking your rights.
  • Severance isn't required by law — it's discretionary.

General information, not legal advice. For your situation, use the official New York resources below or an employment attorney.

Money and leverage while you search

  • Claim unemployment the day you're let go (details above); report part-time or gig income and you may still get a reduced benefit.
  • Use New York's pay-transparency law. Employers with 4 or more employees must include the salary range in job postings (and in promotion and transfer postings). It's one of the broadest in the country — use those numbers to target and negotiate.

Your résumé for the New York market

New York hiring is ATS-first, whether it's a bank, a hospital or a startup. Mirror the posting's exact keywords, keep the formatting clean, lead with achievements and metrics, and skip the photo, birth date and age.

Frequently asked questions

How much is unemployment in New York, and for how long?

New York recently raised its maximum weekly benefit from $504 to up to $869 a week, payable for up to 26 weeks. File through the NY State Department of Labor at dol.ny.gov.

How much notice do I get before a layoff in New York?

New York's WARN Act requires employers with 50 or more employees to give 90 days' advance written notice before a mass layoff, plant closing or relocation — 30 days more than the federal WARN Act.

Do New York employers have to post the salary?

Yes. Employers with 4 or more employees must include the salary range in job postings, including promotion and transfer postings — one of the broadest pay-transparency laws in the US.

When do I get my final paycheck in New York?

By the next regular payday after you leave, including any earned wages you're owed.

Is this legal or financial advice?

No. It's general information, current as of August 2026 and based on the official New York sources on this page. For your own situation, check those sources or a qualified attorney or advisor.
