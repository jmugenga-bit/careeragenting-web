Just laid off in the DC–Baltimore area? Here's your playbook.

The DMV spans three jurisdictions — Washington, D.C., Maryland and Virginia — each with its own rules, plus the country's biggest concentration of federal workers and contractors. This guide sorts it out: what to do first, the money and rights you're owed where you work, and how to line up your next role. Free to use, and honest about information versus advice.

Start here: which rules apply to you

In the DMV your rights depend on where you actually work — Washington, D.C., Maryland or Virginia. The essentials differ by jurisdiction (there's a side-by-side comparison below), and if you're a federal employee, a separate set of rules applies — read that section next.

The first 72 hours after a layoff

  • Get your final paycheck. Timing depends on your jurisdiction — D.C. requires it by the next business day if you're fired; Maryland and Virginia by the next regular payday (see the comparison below).
  • File for unemployment right away, with the jurisdiction where you worked: D.C. DOES (up to $445/week), Maryland (up to $430/week plus a dependent allowance), or Virginia VEC (up to $478/week) — each for up to 26 weeks.
  • Sort out health cover through your jurisdiction's marketplace (DC Health Link, Maryland Health Connection, or HealthCare.gov for Virginia) or continue your plan via COBRA.
  • Don't rush to sign severance — read it, and have it reviewed, first.

If you're a federal worker, read this first

The DC area has the highest concentration of federal employees in the country, and their rules are different:

  • Unemployment for former federal workers runs through UCFE (Unemployment Compensation for Federal Employees) — you still file with the state or district where your job was based (D.C., Maryland or Virginia), using your SF-8 and SF-50 forms.
  • Layoffs (RIFs) and severance follow federal rules, not state ones — reduction-in-force procedures, severance formulas and reinstatement priority are set by the U.S. Office of Personnel Management (OPM). Check your RIF notice and your rights before signing anything.
  • Your next role might be federal (USAJOBS), a contractor, or a move to the private sector — where a long federal résumé needs translating into a shorter, achievement-led one. Wren can help with that.

Your rights across the DMV

  • At-will, with limits in all three — you can't be fired for an unlawful reason such as discrimination or retaliation.
  • Salary now goes in the posting — everywhere in the DMV. D.C. has required a pay range in job postings since June 2024 (any employer), Maryland since October 2024 (with a benefits description), and Virginia joins them from 1 July 2026 (all employers). Use those numbers to target the right roles and negotiate from fact.
  • Layoff notice comes from the federal WARN Act (60 days from larger employers); Maryland adds its own law requiring 60 days' notice from employers with 50 or more staff.

Your résumé for the DC market

DC-area hiring prizes clearances, federal experience and cyber/technical skills — and it's ATS-first. Mirror the posting's exact keywords (including your clearance level where you hold one), keep the formatting clean, and lead with achievements and metrics. One caveat: a federal résumé is long and detailed by design — for private-sector roles, cut it down to a punchy one or two pages.

Frequently asked questions

How much is unemployment in the DC area?

It depends where you worked: up to $445 a week in Washington, D.C., up to $430 a week (plus a dependent allowance) in Maryland, and up to $478 a week in Virginia — each for up to 26 weeks.

Do job postings in the DC area have to show the salary?

Yes, across all three now — Washington, D.C. since June 2024, Maryland since October 2024, and Virginia from 1 July 2026 — employers must include a pay range in job postings.

I'm a federal employee who was laid off — where do I file?

Through UCFE (Unemployment Compensation for Federal Employees), with the state or district where your job was based (D.C., Maryland or Virginia), using your SF-8 and SF-50 forms. Federal RIF and severance rules are set by OPM.

When do I get my final paycheck?

In Washington, D.C., by the next business day if you're fired; in Maryland and Virginia, by the next regular payday.

Is this legal or financial advice?

No. It's general information, current as of August 2026 and based on the official sources on this page. For your own situation, check those sources or a qualified attorney or advisor.
