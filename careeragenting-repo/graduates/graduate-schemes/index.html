Graduate schemes: how to apply and stand out

Graduate schemes are competitive, but they're winnable if you understand how they select — and start early. Here's how the process usually works and where applications are won and lost.

WWren · CV SScout · applications AAce · interviews JJuno · support CCedar · growing in the job

How graduate schemes work

Most UK graduate schemes run a multi-stage process: an online application, often with a few short questions or a CV; online tests (numerical, verbal, situational judgement); one or more interviews (increasingly recorded or one-way); and an assessment centre. Each stage filters, so the goal is simply to pass to the next.

Apply early — timing matters

Many large schemes recruit on a rolling basis and close once they're full, sometimes months before the stated deadline. If a scheme opens in the autumn, applying early in the cycle genuinely improves your odds. Keep a simple tracker of deadlines and where you are with each.

Nail the application questions

Those "Why this company?" and "Tell us about a time you…" questions are where most applications are rejected. Employers want evidence, not enthusiasm. For competency questions, use a clear situation → action → result shape and a real, specific example. For "why us", show you understand what this employer actually does and why it fits you — generic answers are obvious and easy to reject.

Prepare for tests and assessment centres

Practise the online tests in advance so the format doesn't surprise you. For assessment centres, expect group exercises, a presentation, a case study and interviews — they're assessing how you think and work with others, not just the right answer. Frequently asked questions

When should I apply for graduate schemes?

As early in the cycle as you can. Many UK schemes recruit on a rolling basis and close when full — often well before the official deadline — so early applications have better odds.

What is a situational judgement test?

An online test that gives you realistic work scenarios and asks how you'd respond. It's assessing your judgement and fit with the employer's values. Practising the format in advance helps a lot.

How do I answer 'Why do you want to work here'?

Show you understand what this specific employer does and connect it to your genuine interests and strengths. Avoid generic praise — name concrete things about the role, team or company.

Do I need a 2:1 to get on a graduate scheme?

Many schemes ask for a 2:1, but not all — some have dropped strict grade requirements or consider your wider strengths and context. Always check each employer's criteria and don't rule yourself out.
