How to write a graduate cover letter

A good cover letter isn't a summary of your CV — it's a short, specific argument for why you and this role fit. Here's a structure you can reuse and tailor.

WWren · CV SScout · applications AAce · interviews JJuno · support CCedar · growing in the job

What a cover letter is for

It answers three questions the employer is quietly asking: Why this role? Why this company? Why you? Keep it to under one page, three or four short paragraphs, and make every line earn its place. If a role doesn't ask for one, a short, sharp letter can still help you stand out.

A structure you can reuse

  • Opening: the role you're applying for and a genuine, specific reason you're interested — not generic praise.
  • Why you: two or three of your most relevant strengths, each backed by a quick example. Mirror the words the advert uses.
  • Why them: show you understand what this employer actually does and why it fits your goals.
  • Close: a confident, polite sign-off and a thank you.

Tailor every letter

A recycled letter is obvious. You can reuse the structure, but the specifics — the reason you're interested, the examples, the reference to the company — should change every time. Decode the advert for the two or three things that matter most and speak to them directly.

Cover letter mistakes to avoid

  • Repeating your CV word for word.
  • Generic openings that could go to any employer.
  • Making it about what you want, rather than what you offer.
  • Typos and the wrong company name — always check.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a graduate cover letter be?

Under one page — three or four short paragraphs. Make every line count and avoid repeating your CV.

What should a cover letter include?

Why you want this role, why this employer, and why you're a good fit — with a couple of specific examples of relevant strengths, in language that mirrors the advert.

Do I need a cover letter if it's optional?

If it's genuinely optional, a short, sharp, tailored letter can still help you stand out — but a rushed, generic one can hurt. Only include one if you'll do it well.

How do I start a cover letter?

With the role you're applying for and one genuine, specific reason you're interested — avoid generic openings that could be sent to any company.
