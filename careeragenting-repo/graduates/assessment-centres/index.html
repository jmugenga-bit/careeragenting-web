How to pass an assessment centre

An assessment centre feels intense because it's several tasks in one day, watched closely. But once you know what each exercise is really testing, it's far less mysterious. Here's what to expect and how to prepare.

What an assessment centre involves

Held in person or increasingly online, an assessment centre bundles several exercises: a group exercise, a presentation, a case study or written task, one or more interviews, and sometimes a social element. Assessors score you against set criteria across the day, so a wobble in one exercise isn't fatal.

Group exercises: contribute, don't dominate

The group exercise trips people up because they think they must 'win'. They don't. Assessors want to see you listen, build on others, and help the group reach a good outcome. Speak up with useful points, bring quieter people in, keep an eye on the time — but don't steamroll. Being a good collaborator scores higher than being the loudest.

Presentations and case studies

You'll often get a business problem to analyse and present. Structure beats flash: state the situation, your recommendation, and the reasons for it. Manage your time, and don't panic if you can't cover everything — a clear, well-reasoned answer is what they're after.

Interviews and how to prepare

Interviews are usually competency- or strengths-based. Prepare six to eight strong, specific examples using a situation → action → result structure, and be ready to flex them to different questions. Our guide to competency and strengths questions has examples. On the day: arrive prepared, stay calm, be yourself, and treat every exercise as a fresh start.

Frequently asked questions

What happens at an assessment centre?

A day of exercises — typically a group task, a presentation, a case study or written test, and interviews — with assessors scoring you against set criteria. Some include a social element too.

How do I stand out in a group exercise?

Contribute useful points, listen and build on others, involve quieter members, and help the group reach a good outcome on time. Collaboration scores higher than trying to dominate.

How should I prepare for an assessment centre?

Practise online tests, prepare and practise a structured presentation, ready six to eight competency examples, research the employer, and get the practicalities sorted so you arrive calm.

Can I recover from a bad exercise?

Often, yes. Assessors score across the whole day against multiple criteria, so one weaker exercise doesn't necessarily sink you — treat each task as a fresh start.
