How to pass a video or one-way interview

More UK employers, especially for graduate roles, now use video interviews — including one-way ones where you record answers to on-screen questions. They feel odd at first, but they reward preparation.

Live vs one-way interviews

A live video interview is a normal interview over a video call. A one-way (recorded) interview shows you a question on screen and records your answer, often with a time limit and a set number of takes. Both are increasingly common early in graduate processes because they're quick for employers to review.

Get the basics right on camera

  • Look at the camera, not your own face — it reads as eye contact.
  • Good light in front of you, a tidy neutral background, phone on silent.
  • Test your microphone and connection beforehand.
  • Dress as you would for an in-person interview.

Prepare answers, not scripts

For one-way interviews you often can't go back, so practise speaking your answers aloud until they flow — but don't read a script, which always sounds flat. Use the same situation → action → result structure as any competency question, and keep an eye on the time limit.

Practise until it feels normal

The awkwardness of talking to a camera fades with practice. Record yourself a few times and watch it back — you'll quickly spot filler words, pace and energy, and it stops feeling strange. A quick mock run makes a real difference.

Frequently asked questions

What is a one-way video interview?

An interview where you record answers to questions shown on screen, usually with a time limit and a limited number of takes. Employers review the recordings later. Practising out loud beforehand helps a lot.

Where should I look during a video interview?

At the camera, not at your own image — it reads as natural eye contact. Position the camera at roughly eye level.

How do I prepare for a recorded interview?

Test your tech, sort your lighting and background, practise your answers aloud using a situation-action-result structure, and do a couple of recorded run-throughs to get comfortable on camera.

Are video interviews harder than in-person ones?

They're just different. They can feel more awkward at first, but they reward the same preparation — clear examples, good structure, and a few practice runs to get comfortable on camera.
