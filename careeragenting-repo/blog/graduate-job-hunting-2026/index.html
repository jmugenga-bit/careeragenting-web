Graduate job hunting in 2026: how to stand out in a cooler market

If you've graduated recently and the job hunt feels slower than you expected, the data backs up what you're feeling — and, importantly, it also points to what works.

By the Career Agenting System team · 28 July 2026 · 5 min read

The market cooled — but it didn't collapse

Among graduates who finished in 2023/24 and were surveyed about 15 months later, 81% were in some form of work, but full-time employment slipped to 57% — down two percentage points on the year before — and unemployment ticked up to 7%. Analysts described it as “a cooling, not a collapse”: a subdued economy, rather than employers turning away from graduates. (Figures from HESA; full sourcing in our 2026 report.)

What that means in practice: there are still good graduate jobs, but more people are going for each one, so how you apply matters more than it did a couple of years ago.

Your degree gets you in the room; your application gets you the interview

Most graduates applying for the same scheme have broadly similar degrees. What separates the shortlisted from the rest is rarely the classification — it's how well the application is tailored to the specific role.

Mirror the advert. Graduate employers, and the applicant-tracking software many use, look for the language of their own advert. Paste the advert and your CV into our free CV keyword checker to see which key terms you're missing — then add the ones that genuinely apply to you.

Lead with evidence, not adjectives. “Strong team player” means nothing on its own. “Coordinated a six-person group project that finished a week early” means everything. Turn your degree projects, part-time jobs and societies into short, specific examples of what you actually did.

Prepare for the formats graduates actually face

Graduate hiring has its own furniture: online tests, one-way video interviews, assessment centres and competency questions. None of them are as scary once you've seen them coming. Our graduate hub has plain-English guides to each, and our free interview practice tool includes a graduate/first-job set so you can rehearse the likely questions out loud.

If you'd like it done with you rather than by yourself, that's exactly what the free app is for: Wren builds your graduate CV, Scout decodes the advert, and Ace runs realistic interview practice — free every day, no sign-up.

A simple plan for the next fortnight

Pick three or four roles you'd genuinely be glad to get. For each, tailor your CV to the advert's language, prepare six to eight strong examples you can flex across competency questions, and do one full practice run of a video or panel interview before the real thing. Steady and specific beats fast and generic — every time.

The 2026 graduate market rewards preparation. You don't need to be the loudest applicant; you need to be the one whose application clearly matches what the employer asked for.
