Job market

What the 2026 UK job figures mean — and what actually helps

If you're job hunting right now and it feels harder than the headlines suggest, you're not imagining it. Here's what the latest official figures mean in plain English — and what actually helps.

By the Career Agenting System team · 1 August 2026 · 5 min read

We pulled the latest official figures together into one place — you can read the fully-sourced version in The State of UK Job Hunting 2026 — and here's what they mean, plus what to do about it.

The market has tightened

The unemployment rate sounds low at 4.9%, but that single number hides a tighter market underneath. Job vacancies have fallen to around 712,000 — fewer openings, with more people going for each one. And roughly one in five working-age people (an economic inactivity rate of 20.9%) are outside the labour market altogether, neither working nor looking. (All figures from the ONS, mid-2026 — sourced in full on the report.)

What that means for you: competition per advert is up, so a CV that simply lists your duties won't cut through. The applications that get noticed are the ones tailored to the specific role, using the employer's own language.

Young people are feeling it most

More than a million 16–24 year-olds — 1,012,000, about 13.5% of all young people — were not in education, employment or training in early 2026. That's up 89,000 on the year before. Behind each of those numbers is someone at the very start of their working life, often without a CV, a network, or anyone to practise an interview with.

If that's you, the good news is that the basics move the needle a lot at this stage: a clean, honest CV, an application that mirrors the advert, and a few rehearsed interview answers.

What actually helps

None of this is solved by wishful thinking, but three practical things genuinely shift your odds:

Tailor every application. Employers — and the software many use to filter CVs — look for the words in their own advert. Our free CV keyword checker shows you which terms you're missing, so you can add the ones that are genuinely true of you.

Practise out loud. Interviews are a skill, not a personality test. Rehearsing your answers to the common questions is the single cheapest way to come across better. Our free interview question practice tool gives you the questions by type.

Don't do it alone. The help that makes the biggest difference — someone in your corner who knows how to build a CV and prepare you for an interview — is normally expensive or hard to get. If you support job seekers

If you're a work coach, careers adviser or run a job club, the same tools are free for the people you help — and we've made a one-page handout you can print or share. It's built to extend the help you already give, so people make progress between appointments.

The 2026 market is tougher, but it rewards preparation more than ever. Small, specific improvements — the right words, a rehearsed answer, a tailored application — are what get people shortlisted.
