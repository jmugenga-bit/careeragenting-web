Getting back to work

Getting back to work after a long gap: a calm, practical plan

If you've been out of work for a while — after illness, caring for someone, redundancy, raising a family, or a stretch that was just hard — the thought of going back can feel bigger than the job itself. That's normal, and more common than you'd think.

By the Career Agenting System team · 30 July 2026 · 5 min read

Roughly one in five working-age people in the UK are currently outside the labour market. You are not behind, and you are not alone. Here's a calm, practical way through it.

Start with the gap — and take its power away

The single thing people worry about most is “what do I say about the time off?” The answer is simpler than the worry: you don't need to over-explain. A short, honest line is enough — “I took time out to care for a family member and I'm now ready to return” — said without apology. Employers deal with career gaps all the time. What they're really asking is “are you ready and reliable now?” — so answer that.

Rebuild the CV, one section at a time

You don't need to write a CV from a blank page. Start with our free CV template — it's laid out the way UK employers expect, with prompts in each section. Fill in what you've done, including the skills you used while you were out of paid work: organising, budgeting, caring, volunteering, problem-solving. These count, and they belong on the page.

If it helps to have someone do the heavy lifting with you, Wren — our CV specialist — will build it with you for free, turning “what I did” into “what I achieved.”

Rebuild your confidence, gently

Confidence comes back through small wins, not big leaps. Practise saying your two-line introduction out loud until it feels ordinary. Rehearse a few interview answers — our free interview practice tool gives you the common questions so nothing catches you cold. Each small rehearsal makes the real thing less frightening.

And on the days it feels heavy, that's okay too. Job hunting after a gap is emotional work, not just admin. Our companion, Juno, is there for the low moments — not as a therapist or a crisis service, but as a steady, encouraging presence. If things feel genuinely overwhelming, please talk to your GP, or contact Samaritans free any time on 116 123.

A gentle order of doing things

If it helps to have a plan, try this: first, get your two-line “why I'm ready” answer clear. Second, get a basic CV down using the template. Third, pick two or three types of role you'd genuinely take, and tailor your CV to each. Fourth, practise interview answers out loud before you apply. Fifth, apply steadily — a few good, tailored applications beat a pile of rushed ones.

You can read our fuller getting back to work guide for more, and everything we offer is free to start, every day, with no sign-up.

Going back to work after a long time away is one of the braver things a person does. Take it in small steps, be kind to yourself on the hard days, and remember that the gap is a chapter — not the whole story.
